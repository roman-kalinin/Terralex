United Kingdom

What forms of property ownership are available?

Real property in England and Wales can be held as a freehold (an estate in fee simple absolute in possession) or a leasehold (a term of years absolute). Freehold confers the most extensive rights of ownership and is generally indefinite, while leasehold interests are time-limited and subject to the terms of the lease.

How is title to property registered?

Title is recorded at HM Land Registry. Substantially all transactions involving registered land must be registered to take legal effect. The register provides a state-guaranteed record of ownership and any encumbrances such as mortgages and easements.

"Registration at HM Land Registry confers state-backed title guarantee — a key differentiator from civil-law jurisdictions where notarial deeds carry primary evidentiary weight."

What vehicles are commonly used for real estate investment?

Common structures include UK REITs, limited partnerships (frequently English LPs or Jersey/Guernsey LPs), Jersey property unit trusts (JPUTs), and corporate vehicles such as English limited companies or Luxembourg holding entities. Choice is driven by investor profile, tax considerations, and regulatory factors including AIFMD.

What are the principal taxes on real estate transactions?

Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) applies to most acquisitions in England and Northern Ireland, with separate regimes in Scotland (LBTT) and Wales (LTT). VAT may apply at the standard rate on commercial property where the seller has opted to tax. Annual Tax on Enveloped Dwellings (ATED) applies to certain residential property held by corporate envelopes.

What is the typical commercial lease structure?

Commercial leases in the UK are typically full repairing and insuring (FRI) and follow institutional lease principles set by the Code for Leasing Business Premises. Terms are commonly 5–15 years, often with five-yearly upward-only rent reviews and Landlord and Tenant Act 1954 security of tenure unless contracted out.

What is the principal regulatory framework for construction?

The Building Act 1984, the Building Safety Act 2022 and supporting regulations govern building control. The Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015 set health and safety duties. The Building Safety Regulator now oversees higher-risk buildings; planning permission is required separately under the Town and Country Planning regime.

France

What forms of property ownership are available?

French law recognises full ownership (pleine propriété), bare ownership (nue-propriété) and usufruct (usufruit), as well as long-term leasehold structures such as the bail emphytéotique and the bail à construction. Co-ownership of buildings (copropriété) is regulated by the Law of 10 July 1965.

How is title to property registered?

Acquisitions are formalised by notarial deed before a French notary and recorded at the Service de la Publicité Foncière. Registration is necessary for the transfer to be opposable to third parties.

What vehicles are commonly used for real estate investment?

Common French vehicles include the SCI (Société Civile Immobilière), SCPI, OPCI and SIIC (the French REIT). Choice depends on investor profile, regulatory regime and the desired tax treatment.

Germany

What forms of property ownership are available?

German law recognises full ownership (Eigentum), heritable building rights (Erbbaurecht) and condominium ownership (Wohnungs- und Teileigentum). Usufructuary rights (Nießbrauch) and easements (Dienstbarkeiten) can be registered in addition to these primary interests.

How is title to property registered?

Title and encumbrances are recorded in the Grundbuch (land register) maintained by the local court. Transfers require notarisation and registration; the Grundbuch enjoys a public-faith presumption of accuracy.

What are the principal taxes on real estate transactions?

Real Estate Transfer Tax (Grunderwerbsteuer) applies, with rates set by individual Länder ranging from 3.5% to 6.5%. Share-deal rules (Section 1 GrEStG) may also trigger liability where ≥ 90% of shares in a property-holding entity are transferred within ten years.

United States

What forms of property ownership are available?

Real property in the United States can be held in fee simple (the most complete form of ownership), in life estates, by future interests such as remainders and reversions, or under leasehold. Forms of co-ownership include tenancy in common, joint tenancy and (in many states) tenancy by the entirety.

How is title to property registered?

The U.S. uses a recording system (rather than registration of title) administered at the county level. Title insurance is the principal means of establishing the validity of ownership and protecting against undisclosed defects.

What vehicles are commonly used for real estate investment?

Limited liability companies (LLCs), limited partnerships, Delaware Statutory Trusts and Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) are widely used. The choice is driven by liability protection, federal and state tax planning, and the requirements of investors and lenders.
