EU Update —
Legal Insights from Europe's Capital

AM Author Anne MacGregor Published 08 April 2026 6 min read
Share

TerraLex's monthly EU newsletter, developed by Geraint Lewis, brings practitioners across the network a focused brief on the regulatory, policy and policy-adjacent shifts radiating out of Brussels — distilled for legal teams advising clients in Europe and beyond.

The EU Update is a new monthly TerraLex newsletter developed by David Law's EU practice in Brussels, providing timely insights into key EU legislative and policy developments impacting clients doing business across the European Union's 27 Member States and 450 million consumers. This edition offers a concise, practical look at emerging regulations and trends, drawing from foreign-direct-investment reforms and new industrial policy initiatives to GDPR developments and their global implications, helping member firms and their clients stay ahead of an increasingly complex regulatory landscape.

Designed as valuable resource for cross-border advisory work, the EU Update combines high-level analysis with actionable insights, while also connecting members to TerraLex's expert network across Europe for deeper, jurisdiction-specific guidance. Each edition is published with one objective in mind: helping busy lawyers spend less time scanning headlines and more time advising on what those headlines actually mean for clients.

What's in this month's edition

This release of the EU Update collects four threads our Brussels practitioners have been tracking through Q1 and into the spring legislative cycle. Each item is summarized in plain language, tagged by sector, and links through to the underlying primary source — Commission proposal, Council position, or final text in the Official Journal — so you can verify in one click rather than chase citations across three databases.

1. Foreign Direct Investment screening

The Commission's revised FDI screening regulation has now been adopted in trilogue and is moving toward Member-State transposition. The headline change is a tightened scope: more sectors are in, multi-jurisdiction filings are explicitly contemplated, and the cooperation mechanism between national screening authorities gains real teeth. For deal teams, the practical effect is that early-stage deal modelling needs to factor an EU-level review path that did not previously exist as a hard gating item.

2. The new EU Industrial Policy package

The Commission's Industrial Policy package — branded as a competitiveness reset — bundles state-aid flexibility, simplified permitting for strategic projects, and a recalibrated approach to public procurement under the Critical Raw Materials Act. For clients in the energy, automotive, and life-sciences sectors, the package opens fresh public-funding channels but tightens the strings attached to them.

"The next 18 months are going to define how Europe regulates the technologies it imports, the technologies it builds, and the data that flows between them. Member firms with cross-border practices need to be in the conversation now, not after the regulations land." — Anne MacGregor, EU Practice Lead, Brussels

3. GDPR enforcement — what's actually changing

Two years on from the GDPR's "one-stop-shop" reform proposal, we are starting to see how the procedural changes affect outcomes. Cross-border cases that previously stalled at the cooperation phase are now reaching final decisions roughly twice as quickly. The fines themselves are not getting bigger — but the certainty around timing is, which changes how clients should be sequencing remediation, notification, and reserve provisioning.

4. AI Act implementation timeline

The AI Act's tiered timeline is now firm: prohibited-practice provisions are in force, general-purpose-AI obligations apply from this summer, and the high-risk system requirements stage in over the next 24 months. The most practically urgent item for in-house counsel is the governance-and-documentation obligation — the paper trail required for any AI system touching employment, credit, or essential services is substantial, and most companies we see are starting late.

  • Inventory AI use cases now — even the ones procurement classified as "just analytics."
  • Map which use cases fall under the high-risk classification under Annex III.
  • Stand up an AI governance committee with legal, IT, HR, and product representation.
  • Begin building the conformity-assessment documentation; do not wait for the deadline.
